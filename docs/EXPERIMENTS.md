# Post-D2 experiments: results

All numbers come from `results/experiments/*/`, generated by the scripts listed in `docs/REPRODUCIBILITY.md`. The frozen D2 evaluation in `results/metrics/final_test/` was not modified, and its SHA-256 hashes are checked by the test suite. Nothing was fitted, tuned or selected on the held-out test participants S13–S15.

## Phase 1: calibration of the reliability classifier

**Question.** Do the classifier's failure probabilities match how often failures actually happen?

**Method.** Platt scaling and isotonic regression were compared against the original probabilities on validation participants S11–S12, each fitted on one participant and scored on the other. The method with the lowest out-of-participant Brier score was chosen. All calibrators were then refit on S11–S12 together and evaluated once on test. ECE uses 15 equal-width bins. The original classifier checkpoint is unchanged; the calibrators are saved in `checkpoints/experimental/calibration/`.

Validation, out of participant (selection data):

| Method | ECE | Brier | Log loss | ROC AUC |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 0.1201 | 0.2143 | 0.6131 | 0.7323 |
| **Platt (selected)** | **0.0414** | **0.1896** | **0.5513** | 0.7324 |
| Isotonic | 0.0801 | 0.1969 | 0.5953 | 0.7014 |

Held-out test, reported once:

| Method | ECE | Brier | Log loss | ROC AUC | Mean predicted | Observed failure rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original | 0.2242 | 0.1938 | 0.5478 | 0.7308 | 36.3% | 13.9% |
| **Platt** | **0.1560** | **0.1401** | 0.4354 | 0.7308 | 29.5% | 13.9% |
| Isotonic | 0.1610 | 0.1424 | 0.4342 | 0.7309 | 30.0% | 13.9% |

**Reading.** The original probabilities are overconfident about failure. On test they predict a 36.3% failure rate where 13.9% occurs. Platt scaling, chosen on validation, also improves test results: Brier drops 28% (0.194 to 0.140) and ECE 30% (0.224 to 0.156), with ranking (AUC) unchanged. Calibration cannot fully fix the overconfidence because failures are far more common in the validation participants (33.7% of windows) than in the test participants (13.9%), a participant-level shift that a map fitted on S11–S12 cannot anticipate. Per participant, S15 is already well calibrated (ECE 0.06), while S13 and S14 carry most of the error.

Outputs: `results/experiments/phase1_calibration/` (`reliability_diagram_test.png`, `calibration_maps.png`, per-bin and per-participant tables).

## Phase 2: reducing false positives

**Question.** Can the learned agent's false-positive rate (39.6% on test) be lowered while keeping strong recall?

**Selection rule, fixed in advance.** Choose the lowest validation false-positive rate among candidates whose validation recall is at least the original agent's validation recall (85.7%) minus 5 points, so at least 80.7%. Ties go to higher F1. Rewards are always reported under the original reward function.

**Candidate families.** All were scored on S11–S12:

- Calibrated probability threshold, no investigation, τ from 0.05 to 0.95.
- Agent plus calibrated veto: the original agent runs unchanged, and a FLAG is kept only if the calibrated probability is at least τ.
- Cost-based rule: flag when the calibrated probability exceeds the expected-reward break-even point for a given false-positive penalty (no tuning; τ = 0.273 for the original costs).
- Retrained policies: fitted Q retrained on S1–S10 with the original procedure (`src/agents/fitted_q_training.py`) and alternative rewards, three seeds each.

Reward sensitivity, validation, mean ± SD over seeds 42–44:

| Reward variant | Recall | FPR | F1 | Checks / window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Original reward, retrained | 81.2% ± 3.1 | 34.6% ± 2.6 | 0.651 | 1.29 |
| FP penalty −2.0 | 70.8% ± 3.8 | 27.1% ± 0.8 | 0.631 | 1.45 |
| FP penalty −2.5 | 58.9% ± 2.4 | 23.4% ± 0.9 | 0.575 | 1.37 |
| FP penalty −3.0 | 50.7% ± 3.1 | 19.6% ± 0.8 | 0.535 | 1.30 |
| Check cost 0.05 | 78.8% ± 2.0 | 33.8% ± 4.2 | 0.643 | 1.60 |
| Check cost 0.20 | 86.8% ± 1.5 | 41.0% ± 0.7 | 0.649 | 0.41 |

For reference, the production policy on validation scores 85.7% recall and 39.9% FPR.

Held-out test, the best candidate from each family by the validation rule, next to the frozen baselines:

| Policy | Accuracy | Precision | Recall | F1 | FPR | Checks / window | Avg reward |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Frozen: Fixed Rule | 68.3% | 26.1% | 70.3% | 0.381 | 32.0% | 1.04 | −0.404 |
| Frozen: Learned Fitted Q | 64.2% | 26.4% | 88.1% | 0.406 | 39.6% | 0.96 | −0.396 |
| Frozen: No Investigation | 67.4% | 23.3% | 58.7% | 0.333 | 31.2% | 0.00 | −0.355 |
| Calibrated threshold τ = 0.30 | 61.3% | 23.0% | 76.5% | 0.354 | 41.2% | 0.00 | −0.385 |
| Agent + calibrated veto τ = 0.26 | 66.9% | 26.9% | 80.9% | 0.404 | 35.4% | 0.96 | −0.382 |
| Cost-based rule (original costs) | 58.2% | 22.7% | 83.7% | 0.357 | 46.0% | 0.00 | −0.407 |
| Retrained, original reward (3 seeds) | 68.1% | 28.3% | 84.3% ± 0.9 | 0.424 | 34.5% ± 3.8 | 1.28 | −0.383 |

**Reading.**

1. **No variant achieved a strictly better trade-off.** No candidate beats the production agent on both recall and FPR, on validation or on test. Every option moves along the same frontier.
2. **Penalizing false positives works as intended but costs too much recall.** FPR falls steadily as the penalty grows, but recall falls faster. All penalized variants are below the recall floor.
3. **The check cost mainly changes how often the agent investigates.** At 0.2 per check the agent investigates 68% less (0.41 vs 1.29 checks per window) with similar F1.
4. **Training randomness is as large as the effect being sought.** Retraining the original reward with three seeds gives validation FPR from 32.8% to 37.6%. The overall pick under the rule, the retrained original reward, scores lower FPR on test (34.5% vs 39.6%) and higher F1 (0.424 vs 0.406), but with a seed SD of 3.8 points in FPR. That difference reflects training variance, not a better method. The production checkpoint is one draw near the high-recall end of that range.
5. **Investigation adds value.** At equal false-positive rates, the agent-based rules have higher recall than probability thresholds alone (`validation_tradeoff.png`).
6. **The calibrated veto is the best deterministic option if fewer false alarms matter more than recall.** It needs no retraining. On test it gives 4.2 points less FPR (35.4%) for 7.2 points less recall (80.9%), with the same F1 and slightly better reward. It is an alternative operating point, not an improvement.
7. **The cost-based rule underperforms.** Because Platt scaling still overestimates risk on test, the break-even threshold flags too often (46.0% FPR).

Outputs: `results/experiments/phase2_fp_reduction/`. Experimental checkpoints are in `checkpoints/experimental/policies/`.

**Reading.**

1. **The agent has the highest recall in all 18 conditions.** The margin is largest when the pulse signal weakens: at gain 0.25, recall is 72.4% for the agent, 53.5% for the fixed rule and 24.8% for no investigation.
2. **Under heavy noise or motion artefacts the system flags almost everything.** Recall nears 100%, but FPR reaches 85–95% and the agent stops investigating. This fails in the safe direction but loses its selectivity.
3. **Quieter degradations are the main risk.** Baseline wander and attenuation lower recall to 72–75%, so some new failures go unflagged.
4. **The classifier is vulnerable to rhythmic motion.** A strong 2 Hz artefact, similar to a step rate, drops its AUC to 0.41, below chance.
5. **False positives are mostly a motion problem in real recordings.** The agent's FPR goes from 3.0% in the calmest quarter of windows to 76.8% in the most active. 6. **Participant-level variation** (frozen per-participant results, `participant_level_frozen.csv`): agent recall is 94.2%, 82.6% and 88.2% for S13, S14 and S15. FPR ranges from 31.5% for S15 to 48.2% for S14.

## D4: extended evaluation

No model was trained or retrained in D4. The frozen D2 CNN, reliability classifier and fitted Q policy, and the D3 Platt calibrator, are used unchanged. Every choice (motion-level edges, thresholds) was made on validation participants S11–S12; test participants S13–S15 were evaluated once, after selection. Configs: `configs/experiments/d4_motion_thresholds.json` and `configs/experiments/d4_evaluation.json`. Notebook: `notebooks/D4_Extended_Evaluation.ipynb`.

### D4.1 Motion-aware veto thresholds

**Question.** Can separate veto thresholds per motion level reduce false alarms without a large loss of failure detection?

**Method.** Motion intensity is the standard deviation of accelerometer magnitude per window (g). Three levels use the tertiles of pooled validation motion (edges 0.048 g and 0.221 g); test windows use the same edges. The frozen agent runs unchanged, and a FLAG is kept only if the calibrated probability is at least the threshold for that window's motion level (τ = 0.00 means no veto in that level). The comparison is against the frozen agent and the D3 global veto (one τ for all windows). The selection rule and success criteria were written in the config before running:

- Selection: lowest validation FPR with validation recall at least the frozen agent's validation recall minus 5 points (floor 80.7%).
- C1: on validation, the motion-aware veto lowers FPR by at least 1 point compared with the global veto.
- C2: leave-one-participant-out inside validation. The motion edges and both vetoes' thresholds are re-selected on one validation participant and evaluated on the other. This re-selects thresholds only; no model is retrained. In both folds the motion-aware veto must have lower held-out FPR than the global veto and keep held-out recall within 5 points of the frozen agent.
- Motion-aware thresholds are supported only if C1 and C2 both hold.
- C3: test confirmation, reported once and not used for selection.

Selected thresholds: global τ = 0.26; motion-aware τ = 0.56 (low), 0.23 (moderate), 0.20 (high).

| Split | Policy | Recall | FPR | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Validation | Frozen agent (D2) | 85.7% | 39.9% | 0.648 |
| Validation | Global calibrated veto | 81.3% | 36.7% | 0.641 |
| Validation | Motion-aware veto | 80.8% | 34.9% | 0.648 |
| Test | Frozen agent (D2) | 88.1% | 39.6% | 0.406 |
| Test | Global calibrated veto | 80.9% | 35.4% | 0.404 |
| Test | Motion-aware veto | 79.9% | 35.2% | 0.401 |

Leave-one-participant-out check (C2), threshold re-selection within validation only:

| Selected on → evaluated on | Global veto recall / FPR | Motion-aware recall / FPR | Held-out recall floor | Fold |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S11 → S12 | 78.9% / 32.2% | 76.0% / 32.0% | 76.6% | Fail |
| S12 → S11 | 82.3% / 40.5% | 76.3% / 39.2% | 84.5% | Fail |

**Result: not supported.** C1 passed (1.8 points lower validation FPR). C2 failed: the motion-aware veto had lower held-out FPR in both folds but fell below the recall floor in both. C3 also failed: test FPR is only 0.2 points lower than the global veto, and recall (79.9%) is below the test floor of 83.1%. Per motion level on test, the gain comes almost entirely from low-motion windows, where recall falls from 72.6% (frozen agent) to 22.8%; high-motion FPR stays at 75.4% (76.7% for the frozen agent).

Outputs: `results/experiments/d4_motion_thresholds/` (selection, validation and test comparisons, per-level tables, LOSO folds, `per_level_comparison.png`, per-window `test_decisions.csv` used by D4.2–D4.4).

### D4.3 Uncertainty: moving-block bootstrap

**Method.** Consecutive windows overlap by 6 s, so windows are not independent. Blocks of 30 consecutive windows (about 68 s of signal) are resampled within each test participant, keeping each participant's size. All policies are scored on the same resampled windows, so differences are paired. 2,000 resamples, 95% percentile intervals, seed 42. Block lengths of 15, 60 and 120 windows are reported as a sensitivity check.

Paired differences on test, block length 30 (percentage points; F1 × 100):

| Comparison | Recall | FPR | F1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Agent − fixed rule | +17.8 (14.9 to 20.7) | +7.6 (6.2 to 9.0) | +2.5 (1.2 to 3.8) |
| Agent − no investigation | +29.4 (24.5 to 34.2) | +8.4 (6.6 to 10.2) | +7.3 (5.1 to 9.7) |
| Global veto − agent | −7.2 (−8.9 to −5.6) | −4.2 (−4.7 to −3.7) | −0.2 (−0.9 to 0.5) |
| Motion-aware veto − agent | −8.3 (−11.0 to −5.9) | −4.4 (−5.1 to −3.8) | −0.5 (−1.5 to 0.4) |
| Motion-aware veto − global veto | −1.1 (−3.6 to 1.2) | −0.2 (−0.9 to 0.3) | −0.3 (−1.2 to 0.6) |

**Reading.** The agent's recall gain and its higher FPR relative to the fixed rule both have intervals that exclude zero, at every block length tested (15–120 windows). The motion-aware and global vetoes cannot be distinguished. With only three participants, these intervals describe variation within these recordings, not the wider population.

Outputs: `results/experiments/d4_bootstrap/` (`metric_intervals.csv`, `paired_differences.csv`, `paired_differences_by_participant.csv`, `paired_differences.png`).

### D4.4 Failure cases

**Method.** Every test window is assigned to one category from the frozen agent's decision: caught failure, missed failure, false alarm or correct accept. Investigation measurements (pulse spectrum, motion, temporal consistency) are recomputed with the same deterministic tools the agent uses. Representative examples are chosen by a fixed rule: the window closest to the category median of standardized calibrated probability, motion intensity and absolute error.

| Category | Windows | Median p | Share in high motion | Decided without checks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Caught failure | 1,590 | 0.60 | 56.7% | 39.7% |
| Missed failure | 214 | 0.28 | 20.1% | 7.9% |
| False alarm | 4,437 | 0.61 | 45.1% | 49.2% |
| Correct accept | 6,766 | 0.11 | 9.0% | 33.5% |

**Reading.**

1. The classifier is near chance within high-motion windows (AUC 0.53, compared with 0.64 in moderate and 0.89 in low motion). This is why no threshold removes high-motion false alarms.
2. About half of false alarms (49.2%) are flagged without any check.
3. Missed failures are mostly low-probability, low-motion windows that the agent did investigate (only 7.9% were decided without checks).
4. Compared with the probability-only rule (flag if p ≥ 0.5), investigations caught 577 extra failures and avoided 637 false alarms, but raised 1,580 false alarms and caused 45 misses.

Outputs: `results/experiments/d4_failure_cases/` (`category_summary.csv`, `category_by_motion.csv`, `investigation_changes.csv`, `representative_examples.csv`, `representative_examples.png`, per-window `window_findings.csv.gz`).

## Limitations

- Calibration and Phase 2 selection rely on two validation participants. The S11–S12 versus S13–S15 difference in failure rate shows how much participants vary.
- Policy retraining is not bit-reproducible across hardware (see `REPRODUCIBILITY.md`), so differences of a few points between single policies should not be over-interpreted.
- Synthetic perturbations are simplified stand-ins for real artefacts, and their levels are relative to each window's own signal.
- Reward is reported under the project's original reward function, which is one modelling choice among many.
